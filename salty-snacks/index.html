November 24, 2026

California revises its proposed digital-products tax rules — December renewals may stay nontaxable

On November 18, CDTFA issued proposed emergency regulations to implement SB 122, which generally subjects electronically delivered or remotely accessed prewritten software, including SaaS, to California sales and use tax beginning January 1, 2027.

If approved by the Office of Administrative Law, the proposal walks back the September discussion draft’s position that periodic subscription commitments are continuing sales. Instead, each transfer of access rights is treated as a separate sale. A multi-year renewal that transfers access rights immediately and unconditionally in December 2026 would remain nontaxable for its full term, even if payments continue into 2027. Monthly auto-renewals, however, would become taxable beginning with the first renewal on or after January 1, 2027. And a December payment for access that begins January 1 would be taxable.

A few other items in the proposal worth noting: prewritten software with AI is expressly included as a taxable digital product; qualifying IaaS is identified as an example of excluded digital infrastructure, while PaaS is not specifically named; and registered purchasers can issue blanket multiple-points-of-use certificates and assume responsibility for reporting California use tax attributable to their California use.

If you have December renewals, now is the time to review them. The date the access rights transfer, rather than simply the payment date, generally determines whether the sale falls before or after the January 1 effective date. A later payment can still be taxable, however, if it triggers the transfer or expansion of additional rights.

If you provide or purchase enterprise software, please feel free to reach out with any questions.

Source: https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/digital-products-emergency.htm

November 23, 2026

Three States Enacted Prediction Market Taxes in 2026.

Fifteen (15) additional states considered prediction-market bills in 2026 and three fully enacted taxes on these activities this year, including Kentucky, Illinois, and North Carolina.

Illinois taxes each sports-event exchange wager at 1.75%, rising to 3.5% after a licensee’s first 5 million exchange wagers in a fiscal year. North Carolina taxes 6% of net trading fee revenue from sports event contracts, but only when the fee-paying trader is domiciled in North Carolina and physically there at the time of the trade. Kentucky taxes 14.25% of transaction fees, defined as the fee to complete a trade plus the amount a customer pays to buy the contract from the operator.

The litigation picture adds another layer. The CFTC sued Kentucky, arguing the tax is preempted and was designed to drive the platforms out of the state. An October 30 order temporarily barred Illinois from enforcing its licensing rules and related taxes on swaps offered through CFTC-regulated designated contract markets and futures commission merchants. Illinois appealed. North Carolina, for its part, recognizes lawful operation by CFTC-registered and licensed markets that offer residents sports event contracts and comply with federal law.

November 20, 2026

Texas proposes to end sales tax on streaming and data processing services to include SaaS

On back-to-back days, outgoing Texas Comptroller Don Huffines announced two proposed narrowings of the sales-tax base. On November 17, proposed amendments to Rule 3.313 ending the tax on streaming; on November 18, amendments to Rule 3.308 taking hosted technology services out of taxable data processing.

Streaming, old law: since 2014, Texas has taxed Netflix, Hulu, and other subscriptions as “cable television service” under a 1984 statute, Tax Code §151.0033, though no Legislature voted to tax streaming. New: internet video would be neither a cable television service nor a taxable amusement service. One limit: video a cable provider delivers as part of its programming through its cable system stays taxable. The chief revenue estimator projects about $290 million in lost state general revenue in fiscal 2027, rising to $375 million in fiscal 2028, with local collections down $94 million in fiscal 2027 and $123 million in fiscal 2028.

Streaming services, new law: Rule 3.308 would exempt hosted software and SaaS. Today, Texas taxes SaaS as data processing if the software is taxable as tangible personal property and data processing is performed on it in Texas. Under proposed rules, an entire SaaS offering would be non-taxable unless it includes taxable data processing (e.g., processing a customer’s own data in Texas). Example: an Austin firm offering SaaS to consumers to file taxes online would charge no sales tax on the subscription. But a firm that processes third-party data in Texas would be taxable.

November 17, 2026

Illinois and Utah's targeted advertising (i.e., digital ads) tax effective January 1, 2027. Litigation continues across the states.

Illinois’s Targeted Advertising Services Tax takes effect January 1, 2027. It imposes a 10% tax on Illinois gross receipts from targeted advertising services once a provider exceeds $1 million in Illinois receipts during the preceding 12 months. The service is generally sourced based on the location of the person receiving the advertisement.

Illinois is already in court. NetChoice filed NetChoice v. State of Illinois, No. 026CH08791, in Cook County Circuit Court on September 11. The complaint raises many of the same federal and constitutional challenges that recently succeeded in Maryland. The case remains at the pleading stage, and no court has held the Illinois tax invalid. On August 14, 2026, the Maryland Tax Court struck down Maryland’s digital advertising tax in three cases brought by Apple, Google, and Peacock TV.

The tax imposed rates ranging from 2.5% to 10% on companies with at least $100 million in annual global revenue and $1 million in Maryland digital advertising revenue. The court held that the tax violated the Internet Tax Freedom Act, the dormant Commerce Clause, and the Due Process Clause. It ordered refunds, with interest, for the companies’ 2022 tax payments. In the Peacock decision, the court also held that the exemption for certain broadcast and news-media entities violated the First Amendment while rejecting Peacock’s foreign Commerce Clause claim.

These were the first Maryland Tax Court decisions to address the tax’s validity on the merits. The Maryland Supreme Court’s 2023 decision was procedural, holding that the earlier plaintiffs had not exhausted their administrative remedies. Maryland has sought judicial review, so the litigation is not over.

Utah’s 4.7% targeted-advertising tax also takes effect for taxable periods beginning January 1, 2027. The Utah Taxpayers Association filed a constitutional challenge on July 28. Missouri took a different path: voters rejected Amendment 5 on August 4 by 83.3% to 16.7%.

Takeaway: Treat January 1 as both a compliance deadline and a litigation watch. Potentially affected providers should determine whether they exceed the Illinois threshold, evaluate how they will identify Illinois users, register, and model the 10% tax. Illinois excludes certain advertising on interfaces owned or operated by qualifying news-media entities, but businesses should not assume they qualify without reviewing the statutory definition.

November 12, 2026

Texas draws the line on data-center power. A power plant is not exempt data center equipment just because it is adjacent to and primarily supporting a data center.

The Texas Comptroller recently concluded that certain power-generation assets serving a qualifying large data center project did not qualify for the project exemption under Texas Tax Code § 151.3595. However, the same equipment may qualify for the manufacturing exemption under Texas Tax Code § 151.318 when used to manufacture electricity for sale.

The practical takeaway is that ownership, contractual relationships, power-sale arrangements, and the equipment’s actual use may determine whether generation assets qualify for an exemption. Simply locating a power plant within or alongside a qualifying data center project is not enough, and the requirements vary depending on the exemption being claimed.

November 10, 2026

Maryland's multiple points of use exemptions can be used on a blanket basis beginning January 1, 2027.

A multiple points of use, or MPU, certificate allows an eligible buyer to direct a vendor not to collect the full Maryland sales tax on certain digital products and taxable data, information technology, and software services used both within and outside Maryland. MPUs are most commonly used for purchases of SaaS licenses where there are users accessing the software from many different locations.

The buyer instead apportions the use and remits the Maryland use tax directly.

Current procedure: Under the rules in effect since July 1, 2025, a buyer generally must obtain authorization and issue a separate MPU certificate for each transaction. The certificate includes the buyer’s estimate of the portion attributable to use in Maryland.

Beginning January 1, 2027: Chapters 197 and 198 of the Acts of 2026, enacted through SB 644 and HB 933, replace the transaction-by-transaction approach with a vendor-level certificate. An authorized buyer will issue one MPU certificate to each vendor, and that certificate will remain effective for future qualifying purchases.

November 9, 2026

Oklahoma extends exempt-entity sales tax treatment to contractors' qualified purchases.

Effective November 1, 2026, Oklahoma SB 44 adds a new subsection to 68 O.S. § 1356 that extends an exempt entity’s sales tax treatment to contractors and subcontractors purchasing tangible personal property or services to perform a contract with that entity.

Previously, Oklahoma generally did not allow a contractors to receive the passthrough benefit of an exempt entity’s exempt status. Under the new law, a contractor or subcontractor may substantiate an exempt purchase by providing the vendor with:

  • The exempt entity’s Oklahoma Tax Commission exemption letter or card; and
  • Documentation showing the contract with the exempt entity.

The vendor must retain the documentation as support for the exempt sale.

Takeaway: Contractors working for Oklahoma governmental, nonprofit, or other § 1356-exempt entities should update their purchasing procedures and exemption-documentation processes prospectively.

November 6, 2026

Texas: Marketplace fees are one step from nontaxable

If you sell on Amazon, Etsy, or eBay, or your restaurant delivers through DoorDash or Uber Eats, Texas has been taxing your sales twice.

When a customer buys through a platform, sales tax applies to the full purchase price, including the part the platform keeps as its fee. Since Oct. 1, 2025, Texas has also treated that same fee as a taxable “data processing” service bought by the seller. So the seller pays tax again on the same dollars. The Comptroller had been taking this position in audits for years before it was formally written into the rule.

What’s changing: The Comptroller has formally proposed removing the provision that taxes these fees. The proposal makes clear that fees and commissions platforms charge sellers to list items or facilitate sales are not taxable data processing.

Don’t stop collecting yet: The current rule stays in effect until the change is final, so keep remitting for now. The earliest the change can be finalized is Nov. 15, 2026, and it applies from that point forward. The proposal doesn’t say anything about refunds for tax already paid.

Also on the clock: Comments on a companion proposal that would carve out electronic health records and other healthcare technology close Nov. 29.

Missouri says video-game skins aren't taxable — and the marketplace isn't a facilitator

Missouri DOR’s September 24 letter ruling (LR 8408) held that transfers of Counter-Strike digital cosmetic items on a peer-to-peer marketplace aren’t taxable sales under Chapter 144 — electronically stored information isn’t tangible personal property, and no taxable service is rendered. So the platform has no marketplace-facilitator duties, and its paid software subscription features aren’t enumerated taxable services either.

Takeaway: published DOR authority that a purely digital marketplace sits outside Missouri’s facilitator rules — useful backup for anyone advising digital-asset platforms, with the standard caveat that a letter ruling binds only the applicant for three years.

October 9, 2026

Texas Supreme Court: franchise tax is sourced to where title transfers.

In NuStar Energy, L.P. v. Hancock, the Texas Supreme Court held that franchise tax receipts from tangible personal property are sourced where the buyer takes possession and control in Texas, this could be different from the ultimate destination of the property.

NuStar sold bunker fuel delivered at Texas ports to foreign vessels that legally could not burn it in Texas waters, and still lost: “what matters is the customer’s location when the seller surrenders the goods.”

Case: NuStar Energy, L.P. v. Hancock, No. 24-0037 (Tex. Mar. 13, 2026)

October 7, 2026

Washington tiers its B&O tax for service businesses.

It is the anniversary for some major Washington tax changes, so I thought I would make a post about a tax that is often overlooked.

As of October 1, 2025 businesses with $5 million or more of Washington sourced services revenues, measured in the prior year, are subject to a 2.1% Washington B&O tax rate. The state also updated its approach to apply a three-tier structure to determining the rate.

Unlike an income tax, Washington’s B&O tax is imposed on gross receipts rather than net income, meaning a business may owe tax despite its profitability. Effectively, if you have nexus with Washington and are generating revenue in the state, you very likely need to be filing this return.

Services & Other Activities B&O Rates:

  • Less than $1 million: 1.5%
  • $1 million to $4.999 million: 1.75%
  • $5 million or more: 2.1%

For context, Washington also levies a “retailing” B&O rate which is generally 0.471% and a “wholesaling” B&O rate is generally 0.484%, making the 2.1% service-business rate more than four times higher than the traditional rates many businesses associate with Washington’s B&O tax.

As my younger sister would say: “Don’t sleep on Washington B&O.” Trust me, Washington State Department of Revenue remembers every dollar (whether you do or not) and they are very organized to ensure taxpayer compliance.

October 5, 2026

California's SaaS tax rules take shape

The CDTFA released its Sept. 1 discussion paper with draft emergency regulations implementing SB 122. As you are likely aware, this law will treat most forms of prewritten software and SaaS as taxable tangible personal property starting Jan. 1, 2027, with limited exemptions. Sections 1600.2 and 1600.3 address multiple points of use and out-of-state use.

Understanding how to correctly document the multiple points of use (MPU) exemption will be key for many taxpayers. As you may be aware, this relates to the determination of the taxable base based on where users actually reside and access the software.

With the Sept. 24 comment window closed and final regulations expected before year-end, this draft is the closest thing we currently have to the day-one compliance rules. If you are a seller or consumer of software in California, I highly recommend giving this document a read.

Feel free to reach out if you have any questions.

https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/DigitalProducts-Combined.pdf

— in case you are curious —

Regulation 1600.2

(1) A purchaser of a digital product for multiple points of use may use any reasonable method that is consistent and uniform to calculate the measure of tax that fairly reflects the use of the digital product, including non-optional charges (e.g., mandatory maintenance contracts), Inside this State and outside this State that is supported by the purchaser’s available books and records at the time the transaction is reported for sales or use tax purposes...

(2) A reasonable method to calculate the measure of tax that is consistent and uniform includes, but is not limited to, methods based on the number of users or computers inside this State and outside this State.

(3) It shall be presumed that calculating the measure of tax based on the number of users or computer inside this State and outside this State is a reasonable alternative method.

(4) A method of calculating the measure of tax based on the location of the servers where the software is installed is not considered reasonable.

(5) A purchaser of a digital product that knows at the time of its purchase that the digital product will be available for multiple points of use may issue a multiple points of use certificate (MPU certificate) to the seller in conjunction with its purchase.
