November 23, 2026

Three states enacted prediction market taxes in 2026

Fifteen states considered prediction-market bills in 2026, and three — Kentucky, Illinois, and North Carolina — enacted taxes on them. Kentucky taxes transaction fees at 14.25%, Illinois taxes each exchange wager at 1.75% rising to 3.5%, and North Carolina taxes 6% of net trading fee revenue from sports event contracts.

November 20, 2026

Texas proposes to end sales tax on streaming and data processing services to include SaaS

On back-to-back days, outgoing Texas Comptroller Don Huffines proposed narrowing the sales-tax base. Rule 3.313 would end the tax on streaming, and Rule 3.308 would pull hosted technology services and SaaS out of taxable data processing — unless the offering includes taxable data processing, like processing a customer's own data in Texas. The chief revenue estimator projects about $290 million in lost state general revenue in fiscal 2027.

November 12, 2026

Texas draws the line on data-center power. A power plant is not exempt data center equipment just because it is adjacent to and primarily supporting a data center.

The Texas Comptroller concluded that power-generation assets serving a qualifying data center don't qualify for the project exemption just because they sit alongside it — though the same equipment may qualify for the manufacturing exemption when used to manufacture electricity for sale.

Maryland's multiple points of use exemptions can be used on a blanket basis beginning January 1, 2027.

Starting January 1, 2027, an authorized buyer issues one vendor-level multiple points of use certificate to each vendor. That replaces the transaction-by-transaction approach that's been in place since July 1, 2025.

October 9, 2026

Texas Supreme Court: franchise tax is sourced to where title transfers.

In NuStar Energy v. Hancock, the court ruled that franchise-tax receipts from tangible personal property are sourced to where the buyer takes possession and control in Texas. The court's own words: "what matters is the customer's location when the seller surrenders the goods."

Netflix and the Colorado DOR settled, and on July 22 the Colorado Supreme Court dismissed the case with prejudice. That means the 2025 Court of Appeals decision stands — streaming subscriptions are "corporeal" tangible personal property under Colorado's 1935 statute.

October 7, 2026

Washington tiers its B&O tax for service businesses.

As of October 1, 2025, Washington uses a three-tier B&O structure for services: 1.5% on Washington-sourced services revenue under $1 million, 1.75% from $1 million to $4.999 million, and 2.1% at $5 million or more. Revenue is measured in the prior year.
